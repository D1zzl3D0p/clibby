# Natural turns coordinate support and motion

**The repeated tiny turning steps come from the controller's constraints, not a demonstrated biological requirement.** Healthy humans can redirect themselves with a few placements while rotation overlaps braking and support transfer. Quadruped studies likewise show unequal limb roles, changing contact mechanics, and coordination that depends on speed; they do not establish a universal pet turnaround count. Separately, locomotion polish reduced Clibby's navigation speed by tying it to short-leg stride and serialized step duration. Restore the accepted travel speed independently of stride and cadence, then replace repeated yaw catch-up with a finite, support-aware turn plan. The recommendations below are engineering inferences from research and a read-only implementation audit; this report does not claim that the runtime has been fixed.

## Healthy human half-turns need a few placements

In a standing-turn experiment with fifty healthy young adults, the fast 180° condition produced a **median 2.5 steps**, with the slower conditions generally near three. The instructed fast duration was 1.5 seconds. These population measures support a compact maneuver, but do not prescribe an integer count for every person or task. Standing pivots and walking U-turns must remain distinct from wide traveling arcs. ([Standing-turn experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49201-2), [results table](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49201-2/tables/2))

Walking permits either a step turn away from the principal stance side or a spin turn toward it. Hase and Stein observed rapid U-turns that blended braking with rotation and usually preserved walking rhythm. Their spin example retained support on the ball of the axial foot before transferring to the other foot. This is a credible alternative to completely yaw-locked flat soles, although forefoot loading alone does not quantify sole-ground slip. ([Original walking experiment](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/jn.1999.81.6.2914))

Head and pelvis motion also overlap: healthy subjects initially advanced the head relative to the pelvis, coupled the segments through the middle, and stabilized the head toward the end. A small readable head lead followed by overlapping trunk and pelvis rotation is therefore a stronger starting point than either rigid whole-body rotation or a prolonged look followed by foot shuffling. ([Solomon et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16506002/)) Clinical turning-difficulty samples should not define the default energetic character: one comparative study found only pivot-type turns in its healthy young group, while its older difficulty group generally took more steps and almost never pivoted. This does not make healthy older adults pathological. ([Thigpen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11087304/))

## Quadrupeds coordinate limbs instead of repeating yaw rounds

Turning work differs between limbs. In jumping agility dogs, outer limbs produced approximately twice the inner limbs' vertical and lateral forces, and forelimbs contributed greater lateral forces than hindlimbs. This demonstrates task-specific load redistribution; jumping data cannot prescribe ordinary walking timing. ([Söhnel et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34411999/)) Tight walking turns in four horses involved digital-joint rotation, heel-off, and hoof rotation during breakover. This directly challenges an immutable flat support frame, but hoof mechanics cannot supply a dog's paw-swivel allowance. ([Chateau et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15651728/))

Normal quadruped walking also need not hold exactly three feet down continuously. Labrador walking measurements reported mean stance fractions of **55.6% for forelimbs and 50.2% for hindlimbs** at 0.60–0.90 m/s. By comparison, a cycle allowing only one of four legs to swing at any instant requires an average stance fraction of at least 75%; that comparison is arithmetic. ([Labrador measurements](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807137/)) Dog motion capture also found speed-dependent limb coupling. ([Coordination study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2063503/))

**Engineering inference:** organize a pet reversal into a few coordinated fore/hind placement beats with inner/outer roles, modest body bend, and support progressing around a pivot region or short arc. Permit gait-appropriate overlap only with an explicit support contract. A first prototype can replant each paw once with bounded recovery, but **that is an authored placement budget, not a measured animal norm**. No verified ordinary-walk cat, dog, or capybara dataset here establishes a universal 180° step count or head-to-pelvis delay. Specialized flyball turns show overlapping fore/hind contact and rotation, but their box-assisted mechanics must not be copied into casual flat-ground locomotion. ([Flyball study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-78863-9))

## The speed regression changed travel, not just presentation

The [implementation audit](../notes/Natural%20companion%20turning/implementation-audit.md) compared the inspected working tree against HEAD. The original nonwing baseline was **70 cm/s with 240 cm/s² acceleration**; the resident default multiplier of 2.0 made nominal travel **140 cm/s**. The polish instead derived stride as `.32*R`, where `R` is shortest leg reach, then set speed to `stride/(legCount*duration)`, using `.28` seconds for bipeds and `.24` for quadrupeds. At the default multiplier this yielded 36.72 cm/s for Biscuit, 54.95 for Pip, and 24.72 for Mossback: nominal reductions of roughly **74%, 61%, and 82%**. These are parameter calculations, not measured native traversal speeds.

The swing propulsion multiplier `.88+.12*cos(2*pi*phase)` further ranges from .76 to 1, with phase mean .88; torso error above .35 radians suppresses propulsion entirely. Merely restoring `speed=70` while retaining that unnormalized modulation would still change travel distance over time. Historical wing-capable characters had a separate speed rule and require a deliberate compatibility decision. **Engineering recommendation:** restore accepted navigation speed and acceleration independently, keep anatomy-dependent stride/reach/cadence in the gait layer, and normalize any retained phase modulation. Short legs at high requested speed require faster transfers or an intentional gait change, rather than an undisclosed navigation cap. ([Audited formulas and caveats](../notes/Natural%20companion%20turning/implementation-audit.md))

## Replace all-foot yaw clamps with finite support transfers

The audited `yawStepGoal()` limits a selected foot to ±.55 radians of **every other foot**; torso yaw is simultaneously confined by every foot's ±.3-radian interval, including the swinging foot. The largest-error picker therefore advances synchronized feet in whole rounds. Analytically, a full half-turn requires `N*ceil(pi/.55)` placements: **12 for a biped and 24 for a quadruped**, before possible later recovery. Existing swing state can change observed counts. Shortening each manual replant creates faster tapping without reducing these structural rounds. Neither research stream establishes this all-foot heading-spread rule as biological. ([Controller audit](../notes/Natural%20companion%20turning/implementation-audit.md))

**Engineering recommendation:** capture current world contacts and the already lifted trajectory, then plan two principal human placements plus at most one needed alignment step. Rotate through the actual support and transfer weight during the placement. Separate contact position, contact patch, foot yaw, torso yaw, and travel heading; a bounded forefoot pivot or unloading swivel must be represented explicitly rather than silently violating a flat-foot pin. Choose touchdown positions jointly with root/pelvis motion and measured leg reach. For quadrupeds, use the coordinated beats above; long fore/hind spacing makes simply rotating neutral foot homes around a fixed center particularly risky.

Finish by plan progress and tolerances instead of exact yaw equality across all feet. Preserve link-length limits, sole offsets, bend planes, clearance, support, moving-contact ownership, and continuity when targets change mid-swing. Replace tests requiring ≥12 lifts and ≤.55-radian increments with finite placement budgets, reachable contacts, monotonic turn progress, and preserved steady travel distance at each speed setting. If paired swings are introduced, replace the old single-swing assertion with a specific support-geometry check. Visual native review remains necessary; no test or build was run for this report. ([Audit and proposed verification](../notes/Natural%20companion%20turning/implementation-audit.md))

## Conclusion

Treat pace and turn choreography as separate promises to the user. The first is measurable distance over time; the second is a short, readable sequence of support transfers that fits the character's geometry. This lets speed recover without restoring stretched legs, and lets turns become decisive without disguising sliding or unsupported poses. Biological research guides the mechanisms; character-specific visual and geometric checks must establish the final constants.
