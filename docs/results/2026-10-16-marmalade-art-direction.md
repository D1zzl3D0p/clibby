# Marmalade art direction: 3D to pixels

The user preferred the 3D motion in the matched 2D/3D comparison and requested
art-direction work. The brief is refined, lively pixel art with simple core
forms and approximately the current creature pixel density. Kingdom Two
Crowns, Owlboy and Metroid are reference points. High-detail generated concepts
and childish/simple proportions were rejected.

Three original, editable model studies are preserved locally under ignored
`results/marmalade-3d-art-direction-2026-10-16/`:

- **A, lean tabby:** fine limbs, compact wedge head, thin raised tail and sparse
  markings. Eight colours. The slender body exposes the long-legged stance.
- **B, woodland cat:** fuller shoulders and haunches, compact head, cheek planes,
  thicker curved tail and broad tabby markings. Nine colours.
- **C, graphic copper:** angular cheeks and broad facets, hooked tail, slate
  saddle and warm cream accents. Nine colours.

All consume the same recorded 120-frame procedural pose sequence at 15 fps,
rendered on a 96×96 grid. Geometry changes distinguish the studies; they are not
palette swaps. The comparison includes threefold nearest enlargement and native
size samples on light/dark. Exact palette mapping uses binary transparency and
no dithering. Each folder includes recipes, Blender source and rendered frames.
The root assembler verifies frame counts, dimensions, colours, alpha and the
eight-second GIF. Still/contact sheets were visually inspected.

These remain offline art studies using the previous probe's heuristic spatial
mapping of planar paw targets. A proper spatial quadruped rig, turn/contact
polish and native Qt acceptance are still required. No production character or
runtime changes, native performance measurements or battery claims result from
this study.

## Selected direction

The user selected **B, woodland cat**, specifically for its strong feline outline
and potential to translate across a broader range of pets. Preserve the fuller
shoulder/haunch masses, compact head, broad markings and substantial curved tail.
Future feline variants should vary proportions and surface patterns around that
recognizable structure. This is a design foundation, not a requirement to force
every quadruped onto identical anatomy.

The next art pass concentrates on small-scale facial readability: a clear small
eye, short muzzle/nose separation and restrained cheek/ear planes, without
increasing pixel density, enlarging the head or adding fine fur texture. Native
spatial rig integration remains the subsequent engineering task.
